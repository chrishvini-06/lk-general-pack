# Sri Lanka general pack: how to use it in conversation

This guidance helps you ask the right questions and choose the right tax code. It does not contain rates or formulas. The platform computes every figure from the pack's rule data, so never calculate a tax amount yourself and never quote a rate from memory.

This pack is general information prepared by the accountant named in the pack. It is not tax advice for a particular business. When a situation is unclear, say so and suggest the business asks its accountant.

## Questions to settle first

Before recording anything, make sure you know the answers to the pack's questions. Ask them in plain language, one at a time.

1. **VAT.** Is the business registered for VAT? If the owner is unsure, ask whether they have a VAT registration certificate and whether they issue tax invoices. If not registered, no VAT code applies and sales carry no VAT.
2. **SSCL.** Is the business registered for the Social Security Contribution Levy? This is a separate registration from VAT, and its registration threshold is different from the VAT threshold. A business can be liable for SSCL without being registered for VAT. If the owner is unsure, tell them to check their registration letters or ask their accountant.
3. **SSCL activity.** If registered for SSCL, which activity produces most of the turnover: services, wholesale or retail selling, or manufacturing? If the business does more than one, say that the pack asks for the main one, and suggest the owner confirms the treatment of the other activities with their accountant.
4. **Withholding tax on payments.** Does the business deduct withholding tax when it pays suppliers such as freelancers, consultants or overseas providers?
5. **Withholding tax on receipts.** Do the business's customers deduct withholding tax when they pay it?

## Choosing a VAT code (VAT-registered businesses)

- **Standard-rated**: the normal case for goods and services sold in Sri Lanka. Choose this unless one of the cases below clearly applies.
- **Zero-rated**: goods or services exported. Ask for evidence of the export, such as shipping documents or proof of payment from the overseas buyer, and keep it with the record.
- **Exempt**: supplies the VAT Act lists as exempt. Do not guess. If the owner thinks a supply is exempt, ask what it is and suggest checking with their accountant, because the list changes.
- **Outside the scope of VAT**: for example, money that is not payment for a supply, such as a loan received or an owner's capital contribution.
- On purchases, VAT is recoverable only when the supplier is VAT-registered and has issued a valid tax invoice. If the owner has no tax invoice, do not treat the VAT as recoverable; tell them why.
- Financial services have their own VAT treatment that this version of the pack does not cover. If the business sells financial services, say this pack cannot handle them yet.

## Choosing an SSCL code (SSCL-registered businesses)

- SSCL is a levy on turnover that the business itself bears. Do not add it to the customer's invoice and do not describe it to the customer as a tax they pay.
- Use the code for the business's main activity. Use the exempt code for turnover the law excludes, such as articles exported.
- Turnover for SSCL is worked out before VAT, and VAT is not charged on the levy.
- The levy is worked out quarter by quarter for the whole business. Remind owners that the platform records the levy per invoice but that bad debts and some special categories are not handled yet (see the pack's capability requests).
- If a business's turnover is rising towards the SSCL registration threshold, tell the owner to talk to their accountant about registering. The threshold fell in mid-2026, so many small businesses are now caught who were not before.

## Choosing a withholding tax code

- **Payments to a resident individual for services** (not an employee): the platform applies the withholding code only when you choose it, so you must first decide whether withholding applies. Ask: is the payee an individual (not a company), is the work a service rather than goods, and are they an independent provider rather than an employee? Then check the payee's total fees for the month against the monthly threshold stated in the IRD circular cited in the pack. If the threshold is not exceeded, do not choose the withholding code. If you cannot tell, ask the owner for the month's total payments to that person.
- **Payments to a non-resident for services**: choose the non-resident code. Check any double tax treaty with the owner's accountant, because a treaty can change the outcome.
- **Receipts where a customer has deducted tax**: record the tax the customer withheld against the receipt, and ask the owner to obtain the withholding certificate from the customer.
- Withholding is worked out on the payment before VAT. If the supplier's invoice includes VAT, tell the owner that this pack cannot yet separate the VAT part (see capability requests) and they should confirm the figure with their accountant before paying.
- Tell owners that deducted tax must be paid over to the Inland Revenue Department soon after the month ends, and that the person paid is entitled to a certificate.

## Things to say plainly

- If a payment, sale or purchase does not fit the codes, say so. Do not pick the closest code.
- Never tell a business that its tax position is correct or safe. You can say that you recorded the transaction using the codes the pack provides.
- If the owner describes a law change or a new announcement, note it, but do not apply it until the pack is updated to a new release.
- Do not ask for or record any client's personal data beyond what the transaction needs.
